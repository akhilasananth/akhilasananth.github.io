I am still figuring out what I will be when I "grow up".
It feels like a riddle etched into my bones—one I’ll keep deciphering in perpetuity.

I have noticed a common thread in everything I do. I am a curious bug 🐞 who loves to dream and create.
If I had to define myself, I’d say I’m a creative—
and code just happens to be one of the most powerful tools to bring my ideas to life.

But it’s more than just a tool. I stuck with programming for all these years because I genuinely enjoy the craft—
the problem-solving, the structure, and the constant evolution.
I also love that the novelty never wears off—there's always something new to learn.

### Professional Work

I have only worked at one big company in my software engineering career. I did my internship there when I was in school,
got hired when I graduated and worked there for six years.
So here is my 2 cents about my experience there.

At RBC Capital Markets, I built tools—
- to handle large volumes of data and transform them;
- to visualize and interpret information from different data sources;
- and to easily look things up via APIs.

A lot of what I built followed an agile, iterative approach.
I got to interact with users frequently, see the impact of each release, and tweak things.
That part was really fun—especially when users said things like, “Oh, this saves me hours!”.

I primarily worked with Python (Django), JavaScript (React), and Postgres.
I also briefly explored Scala, which challenged me to think differently with its functional paradigm—
and reminded me how quickly I can pick things up when I’m curious.

I lost my software engineering job last year (end of 2024) after working there for over 6 years.

### Post Job-Loss Blues

I hit a wall after that.

I was deeply demotivated. Programming didn’t excite me anymore.

I was also dealing with a lot of other losses personally, and for a few months… I just stopped.

I didn’t build anything. I didn’t “optimize” my time.

I simply existed.
And honestly? It was beautiful. I felt thankful and very privileged to be able to do that. Doing nothing is wildly underrated and hard.

<Image path="images/about/garfield_comic.jpg" alt="Garfield painting" caption="Garfield Comic Strip [1998-01-08]"/>

Then something unexpected happened: I started painting again!

A friend encouraged me to submit my work to an art show where an artist and a poet were paired together.
The artist would visually interpret the poem and weave its words into the piece.

I love poetry, so I said yes.
That was the first time my work was seen beyond my own walls. It felt nice—and my wonderful friends came to support me 💕.

It sparked something inside me—like striking a matchstick against the side of a box.

That was my re-entry point.

<Image path="/images/about/xkcd_comic_art_project.png" alt="xkcd art project" caption="xkcd - Art Project"
       sourceLink="https://xkcd.com/1496/"/>

I created an Instagram account: <a href="https://www.instagram.com/akhilascope/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">@akhilascope</a> - like a kaleidoscope 😁. I painted more regularly and mustered up the courage to share my paintings with the world.
Before, I’d say things like, “Oh, I paint sometimes, whatever.”
But now, I introduced myself as an artist—because I am one.

PS. I do need to practice to be a <u>good</u> artist 😝

I started going to gallery openings and I met other artists.

I reconnected with a part of myself I had shelved in the pursuit of “being serious”.

Even as a kid, I remember being challenged by my math teacher to explain the formula for the volume of a cylinder: π × r² × h.
I locked myself in a room, cut up paper, and obsessed over it until I built a simple 3D explanation. The next day,
I presented it. I was told that explanation was too simple; of - course I was 12, I am proud of that moment regardless.

I think about that moment sometimes—
**it was creative, nerdy, and deeply me**.

That’s the core I’m reconnecting with.

I serendipitously stumbled upon a creative coding meetup in an attempt to find something fun to do with a friend. It fueled my creative fire and got me excited about coding again!
It reminded me why I got into software engineering in the first place - I wanted to make whatever I imagined.

For the longest time, I was stuck in this loop of asking myself - “What’s next? What’s the right roadmap for me as a software engineer? What are the
rules?”

That suffocated me! 😣

Following roadmaps doesn't work for me I guess, because I pave my own path 😉.

### So Now What?

✨**I want to find a job as a software engineer soon (preferably in the NYC area).**✨

As I mentioned above, in my last job I built tools. Do I want to keep building tools?

**Yes**, absolutely!

But I’m not sure if I want to be *“the gal who builds tools”* yet.

My brain is wired to solve problems —like a cog in a machine surviving on being useful.

At the moment, I am not part of a corporate machine. I get to play and not just survive 🦋

So, I am enjoying my play-time 🛝

I’m reprogramming myself to simply make space for *practice*.

The focus isn’t on chasing an outcome —it’s on showing up every day and creating whatever sparks my curiosity.

It’s a shift in mindset.

Breaking out of that straitjacket has helped me think bigger — not in months or years, but in nodes and goals.

Each node can be a moment, a project, a city or a spark. My current node connects to a few others, and I’ll figure out the next one when I get there.

It sounds like I am lost 😂. I am not.

In the past when it seemed like I had my life together, I was just wandering. I had very vague goals and some of them were so grandiose that it didn't even make sense. One can't possibly be lost while wandering; as they don't have a destination in mind.

Now, I have better goals and know where I want to be.

I’m figuring out the directions at every intersection (or node)—
like standing in a foreign train station, scanning signs in a language I barely know. Confused, a little overwhelmed—but still moving towards a destination (goal).

And if I end up in a place that doesn't feel right, I know that I'll find my way.

The comic strip above depicts something that actually happened to me while I was solo traveling in Japan.
I was on my way to spend the night at a Buddhist temple in a village near Nara.

It was getting late, and I accidentally boarded an express train instead of the local one 😬.
I got off and was stranded alone in a train station with a creepy guy.
I pretended to be on a phone call until a train going in the opposite direction arrived.

Eventually, I made it to the right stop and used my very limited, internet-learned Japanese to get a cab to the temple.
It was pitch dark, and the temple sat quietly on a hill. I didn’t freak out (okay, maybe just a little on the inside),
but I got myself exactly where I planned to go. 💕

Right now, I’m traveling light—literally and figuratively. Living out of a suitcase and two backpacks, changing countries every month.

Shedding clutter, distractions, and the pressure to have everything figured out.

I have goals, but a lot of the details are still blurry—

like testing lenses at the optometrist—

I’m clicking closer to clarity. 🤓
