### Professional Work

I have only worked at one big company in my software engineering career. I did my internship there when I was in school, got hired when I graduated and worked there for six years.

So here is my 2 cents about my experience there.

At RBC Capital Markets, I built tools:
- to handle large volumes of data and transform them,
- to visualize and interpret information from different data sources,
- and to easily look things up via APIs.

I primarily worked with Python (Django), JavaScript (React), and Postgres. I also briefly explored Scala, which challenged me to think differently with its functional paradigm and reminded me how quickly I can pick things up when I’m curious.

### Post Job-Loss Blues

I hit a wall after that.

I was deeply demotivated. Programming didn’t excite me anymore.

I was also dealing with a lot of other losses personally, and for a few months… I just stopped.

I didn’t build anything. I didn’t “optimize” my time.

I simply existed and was depressed.

<Image path="images/about/garfield_comic.jpg" alt="Garfield painting" caption="Garfield Comic Strip [1998-01-08]"/>

At some point, I just wanted to do something. Anything… so I started painting again.

A friend encouraged me to submit my work to an art show where an artist and a poet were paired together.

The artist would visually interpret the poem and weave its words into the piece.

I love poetry, so I said yes.

That was the first time my work was seen beyond my own walls. It felt nice and my wonderful friends came to support me 💕.

It sparked something inside me, like striking a matchstick against the side of a box.

       sourceLink="https://xkcd.com/1496/"/>

I created an Instagram account: <a href="https://www.instagram.com/akhilascope/" target="_blank"
rel="noopener noreferrer">@akhilascope</a> - like a kaleidoscope 😁. I painted more regularly and mustered up the courage to share my paintings with the world. Before, I’d say things like, “Oh, I paint sometimes, whatever.”
But now, I introduced myself as an artist—because I am one. I wouldn’t say that I am a <u>good</u> artist yet, that will take a lifetime!

I reconnected with a part of myself I had shelved in the pursuit of “being serious”. I don’t feel like it’s a waste of time to build something that has no purpose. It’s ok if it’s just cute or whimsical.
